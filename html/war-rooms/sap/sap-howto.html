What's in the SAP suite, and how to use it

Unlike a single-property vertical, the SAP suite is a sequential business flow — Commerce → CRM → CPQ → SD — not a set of independent domains. Master Data, Approval, Governance, and Integration Hub sit underneath and around that flow rather than inside it. This guide covers each war room, how they relay into the SAP Strategist, and how that connects up into the enterprise-wide Digital Twin.

The Business Flow Catalog CRM CPQ O2C Approval / Governance / Integration / MDM SAP Strategist Digital Twin Data & Relay Flow Why This Matters

The Business Flow order-to-cash, not independent demos

Each SAP war room represents a real stage in a quote-to-cash process. A problem at one stage — a duplicate customer record, an incomplete product record — can propagate downstream into a live quote or order. The Strategist renders this as a chain, not a grid, because that's how the business actually experiences it.

Catalog /html/war-rooms/catalog/catalog-war-room.html

Represents SAP Commerce Cloud / Hybris — the product and pricing front end. Tracks SKUs, stock levels, compliance flags, and end-of-life items. This is the front of the SAP chain: bad data here (or in MDM underneath it) is the earliest point a downstream quote can go wrong.

CRM /html/war-rooms/crm/crm-war-room.html

Represents SAP CRM / C4C — customer and opportunity management. Cases and opportunities flow from here into CPQ once a customer is ready to be quoted.

CPQ /html/war-rooms/cpq/cpq-war-room.html

Configure-Price-Quote. Tracks open quotes, pipeline value, discount levels, and SLA breaches. When a discount needs sign-off, the quote is tagged NEEDS APPROVAL — that tag now links straight to the Approval Center instead of being a dead-end label.

O2C — SD /html/war-rooms/o2c/o2c-war-room.html

Order-to-Cash / Sales & Distribution. Tracks orders through credit check, fulfillment, shipping, and AR. When an order stalls past its SLA, the breach now links out to whichever war room actually owns fixing it — the Approval Center for a credit-check stall, or Integration Hub for anything that looks like a system/carrier handoff issue.

Approval / Governance / Integration Hub / MDM supporting engines

  • Approval Center — the real backend behind CPQ's "Approval Workflow (Release Strategy)" and O2C's credit-check stage. Maps to SAP's Release Strategy engine.
  • Governance — maps to SAP GRC (controls, risk, audit). Not central to the order-flow narrative, but part of enterprise-readiness coverage.
  • Integration Hub — the one place that shows which system-to-system interface actually broke, rather than the chain just going quiet. Maps to SAP PI/PO or CPI monitoring.
  • MDM — Master Data Management. The data-quality layer underneath Catalog and CRM. This is where the flagship scenario (see below) originates.

Digital Twin /html/war-rooms/digital-twin/digital-twin.html

The enterprise-wide health aggregator. It already watched BPO, O2C, CPQ, CRM, Approval, Governance, and Integration Hub — it now also watches Catalog and MDM, closing the gap at both ends of the SAP chain: the front-end commerce layer and the data-quality layer underneath everything. Same aggregator pattern as the SAP Strategist, wider scope.

Data & Relay Flow how it connects

  • War room → Strategist/Digital Twin: each war room writes its own domain-scoped relay payload (KPIs, an explain[] array with a per-item severity of high/med/low, and a timestamp) via window.TSM.relay.write(domain, payload), which lands under a shared storage key from relay.core.js's RELAY_REGISTRY (e.g. TSM_CATALOG_RELAY, TSM_MDM_RELAY).
  • Aggregators just read, never recreate: the SAP Strategist and Digital Twin both read those same relay keys rather than re-deriving the data, so a war room's live analysis shows up in both views without extra wiring.
  • Cross-links, not dead ends: stage-stall states (CPQ's "Needs Approval," O2C's SLA breaches) now link directly to the war room that owns resolving them, instead of sitting as static labels.

Why This Matters

The SAP suite isn't four independent demos plus a dashboard — it's a connected operational intelligence layer: war rooms produce signals, the Strategist and Digital Twin consume them from different vantage points, and stalled stages point to whoever actually owns the fix. The point isn't to recreate SAP; it's to give a decision-maker a cross-system view of where a problem originates, how it propagates, and which system or team should act next.
